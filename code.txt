The effectiveness of drug discovery depends on the accurate prediction of protein-ligand binding affinity, and the introduction of deep learning techniques into this field can accelerate the research and development process while reducing resource investment. In recent studies, convolutional neural networks have been utilized by researchers to predict protein-ligand binding affinity, and this approach has demonstrated outstanding performance in relevant tasks. However, this remarkable performance is largely ascribed to convolutional neural networks’ inherent ability to capture the natural representation of biomolecules, rather than a systematic and rational modeling of the intricate interaction mechanisms within protein-ligand complexes. In this regard, we have developed an Equivariant Quantum-inspired Convolutional Neural Network (EQCNNet)-a model capable of learning the 3D geometric structural information of protein-ligand complexes while simultaneously perceiving the node-level interactions associated with protein-ligand binding affinity. This innovative model integrates RBFs with Equivariant Neural Networks(E3NN) to capture radial and angular molecular features: RBFs quantify interatomic distances, Equivariant Neural Networks (using spherical harmonics) ensures rotation-translation invariance, and Clebsch–Gordan coefficients fuse angular and atomic properties. Combining these, our approach efficiently captures molecular rotational symmetries and interatomic interactions. Demonstrated on two benchmark sets, EQCNNet presents exceptional performance and generalization, highlighting the importance of accurate interaction modeling in drug discovery.
